The Puja is the public meeting-place.

This Sharadiya, the Puja is the public meeting-place: neighbourhood, clay, dhak, food, homecoming. Neighbours in Kolkata and Bengalis living away from Bengal can enter the gathering here. Durga Puja in Kolkata is a living civic festival of worship and art. Bharatiya Krishak Samaj Pujo keeps that life at the centre.

Why this Puja

Many Kolkata Pujas stage cinema, architecture, politics or spectacle. This one places the farmer - the annadata - in the gathering, then shows Integrated Farming as a livelihood people can understand, and points to a live demonstration being built at the same place.

The emotional centre remains the Puja. The farming story follows from the gathering; it does not replace it. You do not have to become an agriculture expert to belong here. You only have to arrive as a visitor - and leave knowing why the farmer is in the gathering.

Worship, craft, food, music, neighbourhood

The Puja is the centre. Farmer and farm follow after people have a place to stand.

Worship

The week remains Durga Puja: the mother goddess, the family of deities, the neighbourhood mandap, the days from Mahalaya toward immersion. Come for darshan and the shared week. Detailed ritual timings will be announced shortly.

Craft

Clay, light, bamboo, cloth, paint - the making of the image and the mandap. Photographs from Durga Puja Mahotsav 2025 show last year's craft and gathering. They are not photographs of the 2026 Bharatiya Krishak Samaj pandal unless a caption says otherwise.

Food

Puja weeks are also food weeks: bhog, neighbourhood stalls, shared plates. Expect the warmth of the Bengali festival table when the week arrives. Specific menus and stall lists will be announced shortly.

Music

Dhak, community song, and the sound of the neighbourhood walking. Music remains part of why people come. Artists and show timings will be announced shortly.

Neighbourhood / Community

Durga Puja in Kolkata is famously public: crowds walking together, neighbourhood lines softening for a few nights of shared looking. That civic character is why a farmer-centred story can sit inside the week without becoming a closed conference.

The gathering names the farmer.

The gathering names the farmer who grows food, so the person who feeds the household is seen - not left unnamed. An award ceremony on Ashtami evening recognises the unnamed farmer. Nominations are open. There is no entry fee. You may nominate a farmer you know, or nominate yourself if you are the farmer.

Integrated Farming, in brief

Once the farmer is in view, the next question is livelihood. See how a farm can connect crop, livestock, water and market - so what leaves one part of the system can become useful to another. This public experience keeps that explanation short.

See the farm model on the Farmers / FarmTech + AgriTech door

This is still a Puja

It is not an agriculture conference. The farmer belongs because the gathering names the person who grows food. The farm is what can be shown after that naming.

The larger story

Six steps. Each one follows from the last.

  1. The Puja

    A gathering people can join.

  2. The farmer

    The annadata comes into view.

  3. Recognition

    Awards for the unnamed farmer.

  4. Livelihood

    Why Integrated Farming follows.

  5. Demonstration

    A live farm being built at the Puja venue.

  6. The seed

    This Puja is also the public beginning of a larger invitation: about 5,000 village integrated farms as a vision to work toward. Rs 1 lakh is proposed seed for one village farm - or Rs 20,000 every two months. That is a horizon, not money already collected. Nothing here takes money.
