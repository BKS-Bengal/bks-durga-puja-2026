Worship, craft, food, music, neighbourhood

The Puja is the centre. Farmer and farm follow after people have a place to stand.

Worship

Darshan, aarti, and the days of Sharadiya stay the meeting-place. This gathering does not rewrite Saptami, Sandhi Puja, or visarjan as an advertisement. Times for Anjali, Sandhi, and visarjan are to be announced from a named Panjika.

Craft

Images are unfired clay, traditionally from the Ganga, made in artisanal workshops. Eyes are painted from Mahalaya. The image returns to water on the tenth day. This page does not name a kumor or claim a Bharatiya Krishak Samaj workshop.

Food

Bhog is food offered and then shared. Community kitchens often serve khichuri, mixed vegetables, fries, chutney and payesh. Whether this gathering will serve bhog is not decided here. No menu is invented.

Music

Dhak is named in Kolkata’s Puja heritage. Dhakis keep that sound. This page does not autoplay audio and does not exhibit an unnamed drummer.

Neighbourhood

Household Pujas and public barowari gatherings already coexist in the city. A theme-pandal is not the same as a district gathering. This page will not use committee words until a real committee chooses them.

In December 2021, Durga Puja in Kolkata was inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. That inscription is Kolkata’s Puja, not this page and not an unnamed venue.

The Puja gathers people. Then the farmer is seen. Then a farm can be shown.

Six steps. Each one follows from the last. You do not need insider language to follow them.

  1. The Puja

    This Sharadiya, the public meeting-place is still the Puja: neighbourhood, clay, dhak, food, homecoming.

  2. The farmer

    The gathering names the annadata, the farmer who grows food, so the person who feeds the household is seen.

  3. Recognition

    An award ceremony on Ashtami evening is meant to recognise unnamed farmers. Nominations on the main site have no entry fee as published.

  4. Livelihood

    Once the farmer is in view, the next question is livelihood. Integrated Farming keeps crop, animals, water and market on one holding, so leftover becomes input.

  5. Demonstration

    That model is being built as a working farm in the East Kolkata Wetlands, 500 metres from Sector V. Status: being built. It is not presented as finished.

  6. A longer aim

    This Puja is also the public beginning of a mobilisation target: about 5,000 village farms. That figure is a goal, not money already collected.

Bharatiya Krishak Samaj Awards

The gathering is meant to name farmers Bengal does not usually photograph. Nominations are open on the main Pujo site. There is no entry fee as published. This is not a paid contest. Jury lists are not invented here.

Why a farm follows the Puja

Recognition is not a substitute for a working farm. It is why a livelihood is allowed to stand next to the festival.

Integrated Farming, in plain language

On a small holding, one standing crop often carries the whole year’s risk. An integrated farm keeps several enterprises on the same land so leftover becomes the next input: pond silt to beds, residue to animals, manure to soil.

The model already written for this project is a closed loop on about 1 to 2.5 acres. Mix, scale and results vary. It is a working sketch, not a guarantee of anyone’s income.
