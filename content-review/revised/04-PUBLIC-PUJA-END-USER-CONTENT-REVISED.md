## Main Narrative

### The Puja

This Sharadiya, the Puja is the public meeting-place: neighbourhood, clay, dhak, food, homecoming. Neighbours in Kolkata and Bengalis living away from Bengal can enter the gathering here.

Durga Puja in Kolkata is a living civic festival of worship and art — clay images, pandals, drumming, walking crowds, shared evenings. Bharatiya Krishak Samaj Pujo keeps that life at the centre.

### Why this Puja

Many Kolkata Pujas stage cinema, architecture, politics or spectacle. This one places the farmer — the annadata — in the gathering, then shows Integrated Farming as a livelihood people can understand, and points to a live demonstration being built.

The emotional centre remains the Puja. The farming story follows; it does not turn the mandap into an agriculture conference.

### Worship

The week remains Durga Puja: the mother goddess, the family of deities, the neighbourhood mandap, the days from Mahalaya toward immersion. Exact ritual timings will be announced through official visit channels.

Come for darshan and the shared week.

### Craft

Clay, light, bamboo, cloth, paint — the making of the image and the mandap. Photographs from Durga Puja Mahotsav 2025 (IIT Kharagpur Research Park — KarmYog for the 21st Century Foundation) may appear as **historical / reference** images of craft and gathering. They are last year’s work — not photographs of the 2026 Bharatiya Krishak Samaj pandal unless a caption clearly says otherwise.

### Food

Puja weeks are also food weeks: bhog, neighbourhood stalls, shared plates. Specific 2026 menus and stall lists will be shared when announced. Expect the warmth of the Bengali festival table when the week arrives.

### Music

Dhak, community song, and the sound of the neighbourhood walking. Programme artists and show timings for 2026 will be announced through official channels. Music remains part of why people come.

### Neighbourhood / community

Durga Puja in Kolkata is famously public: crowds walking together, neighbourhood lines softening for a few nights of shared looking. That civic character is why a farmer-centred story can sit inside the week without becoming a closed conference.

### Farmer

The gathering names the farmer who grows food, so the person who feeds the household is seen — not left unnamed.

**Recognition:** an award ceremony on Ashtami evening recognises the unnamed farmer — people Bengal does not usually photograph. Nominations are open on the public surface. There is **no entry fee**.

You may nominate a farmer you know, or nominate yourself if you are the farmer. Scale is not the only test: a small holding with a genuinely better method matters here.

### Integrated Farming

Once the farmer is in view, the next question is livelihood. Integrated Farming keeps crop, animals, water and market on one holding, so leftover can become input — a model a small farm can run.

This public experience keeps that explanation short. The deeper farm model lives on the FarmTech + AgriTech / Farmer door.

### The larger story

Six steps, each following the last:

1. **The Puja** — a gathering people can join.
2. **The farmer** — the annadata comes into view.
3. **Recognition** — awards for the unnamed farmer.
4. **Livelihood** — why Integrated Farming follows.
5. **Demonstration** — a live farm being built.
6. **The seed** — this Puja is also the public beginning of a mobilisation **target** of about 5,000 integrated farms. ₹1 lakh is **proposed** seed for one village farm — or ₹20,000 every two months — with one NRB / supporter patron each, from Puja 2026 to Puja 2027. That is a goal, not money already collected. Payment is not taken through this experience.

## Why It Matters

If you come for the Puja, you should feel the week: worship, craft, food, music, neighbourhood. If you stay with the story, you will also understand why the farmer belongs at the centre — and how Integrated Farming continues after immersion.

That is the invitation: experience the Puja fully, then recognise the livelihood story it carries.

### What a visit can feel like

A good visit starts with the senses: clay and light in the mandap, dhak in the lane, bhog and shared walking. Then, if you read the theme and the awards story, you meet the farmer who is usually left unnamed. Then, if you wish, you can understand Integrated Farming in one clear beat — and know that a live demonstration is being built so the idea can eventually be walked.

You do not have to become an agriculture expert to belong at this Puja. You only have to arrive as a visitor — and leave knowing why the annadata is in the gathering.
