## Why Integrated Farming?

One standing crop — often paddy — can carry the whole year’s risk. Market price, weather, flood, delayed rain and bought inputs can hit that one crop all at once. Between harvests, cash can run thin.

Integrated Farming answers with a different shape: several enterprises on the same land, so work, water and cash can move through the year. The useful idea is simple — **leftover becomes input**.

This is not a claim that every farm becomes zero-waste by magic. It is a way of designing the holding so one activity can feed another.

## The Integrated Farm

An illustrative Bharatiya Krishak Samaj model for about **1 to 2.5 acre** holdings (mix, scale and results vary) is built around five pillars already on project file:

**A — Aquaculture and water harvesting**  
A pond as rainwater store and fishery — native carps such as Rohu, Katla, Mrigal and other indigenous fish where suitable. Nutrient-rich pond water can support careful irrigation.

**B — Bund and embankment multi-tier horticulture**  
Raised bunds planted in tiers: canopy trees (for example coconut, betel nut, drumstick, papaya), climbing vegetables on trellises, and ground crops such as turmeric, ginger and leafy greens.

**C — Livestock and poultry**  
Small dairy units and poultry / duckery beside or over the pond where layout allows. Duck–fish and manure–compost relationships are part of the model sketch.

**D — Bio-inputs and soil regeneration**  
Composting, vermicompost, and related bio-input work so biomass returns to soil rather than leaving the farm as unused waste.

**E — Careful technology (FarmTech + AgriTech layer)**  
Soil moisture sensing and drip, solar pond aeration, crop health diagnostics and yield tracking where they help — **model-based**, not a promise that every tool is already installed on every farm.

## Crop

Cereals, pulses, oilseeds and fodder on the holding. Residue is not only “waste”: it can become livestock feed or compost. Grain and marketable surplus move toward household and market.

## Horticulture

Vegetables, fruit and homestead trees on the same land — including multi-tier planting on pond bunds. Fresh produce can support weekly household and market cash where markets exist.

## Livestock

Dairy and related animals. Manure and leftover feed return to compost and soil. Milk can support daily household cash in a well-managed unit. Scale in the model sketch is small (for example a few cows) — not a large dairy factory claim.

## Poultry

Poultry and duckery as part of the loop. Eggs and birds can support frequent cash. Droppings and leftover feed return into the pond–soil system where designed that way.

## Fishery

Ponds and, where practised, rice–fish patterns. Fish harvests can support monthly or seasonal cash. Pond silt returns to fields as a nutrient source in the model loop.

## Aquaculture

Aquaculture is the water–fish enterprise at the centre of pillar A: harvest + water storage + nutrient exchange with horticulture and livestock layouts.

## Water Harvesting

The pond is both water bank and production unit. Water supports fish, ducks, horticulture and crop irrigation. This is resource design — not a claim of flood immunity.

## Compost

Crop residue, manure and biomass move into compost and bio-slurry pathways so bought fertiliser pressure can fall where the loop is well managed. Outcomes vary.

## Soil

Soil receives compost, bio-slurry and pond silt. Soil health is a central reason the model exists. Regenerating soil is work over seasons — not a one-week festival claim.

## Bee

Beekeeping (apiary) appears in allied livelihoods where flowers and management allow. Honey and pollination support are possible additions — not mandatory on every holding.

## Mushroom

Mushroom units appear as allied enterprise in many Integrated Farming sketches. They can use agricultural residues as substrate. Suitability depends on space, skill and market.

## Household

The farm feeds the household first: food, fuel where biogas is used, and cash for daily needs. The centre of the loop is the farm household.

## Market

Surplus milk, eggs, vegetables, fish, fruit and grain move to market. Market access is a real constraint in many places; this page does not invent a guaranteed buyer.

## Allied Livelihoods

Bee, mushroom, boundary plantations, kitchen greens and similar units sit beside the core loop. They are optional strengths — not a forced checklist for every farmer.

## The Circular Farm Loop

Plain relationships already on project file:

| From | To | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Crop residue | Livestock / compost | Straw and stalks can feed animals or beds |
| Manure | Energy / organic fertiliser | Biogas where installed; slurry to soil and pond plankton |
| Pond silt | Fields | Nutrient-dense silt to vegetable and crop beds |
| Horticulture leaves / greens | Fishery / poultry feed | Surplus greens and azolla can supplement feed |
| Water | Crop, fish, horticulture | One water system serving several enterprises |
| Produce | Household and market | Food and cash returning to the family |

**Honest limit:** “Closed loop” is the design intention. It is **not** a guarantee of zero waste or of fixed income.

**Illustrative / model-based figures** in project material (for example 365-day cash-flow sketches; 3×–5× income ranges; 50–70% cost-reduction sketches) must stay labelled as **illustrative / model-based**. Actual outcomes vary by farm size, location, management, crop mix, input costs, yields and markets.

## FAQ

### What is Integrated Farming in simple words?
Several farm activities on the same land, designed so leftover from one can help another — crop, animals, water, compost, market and household together.

### Is this only a Puja slogan?
No. The Puja names the farmer. This page explains the livelihood model and the demonstration being built.

### Will I get ₹1 lakh if I express interest?
No. ₹1 lakh is proposed seed-support language in the wider mobilisation model. It is not a farmer grant paid on this page.

### Am I enrolled when I submit the form?
No. The form downloads a local file. It is interest, not enrolment.

### Are the 365-day / 3×–5× / 50–70% figures guaranteed?
No. They are illustrative / model-based. Outcomes vary.

### How much land does the model assume?
Illustrative sketch around about 1 to 2.5 acres. Mix and scale vary. Small and marginal holdings are the reason the model exists — not a hard cut-off invented here.

### What is FarmTech and AgriTech here?
Careful tools inside the farm: monitoring, drip, pond energy, diagnostics. Not Palm Tech. Not a vendor list.
