## 2. Cultural context (A)

**VERIFIED.** ‘Durga Puja in Kolkata’ is inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (16.COM, December 2021). [SRC-UNESCO-001] [SRC-UNESCO-002] [SRC-PIB-001]

UNESCO describes an annual festival in September or October; ten-day worship of the Hindu mother-goddess Durga; unfired clay images from Ganga clay; worship beginning on Mahalaya when eyes are painted; ending on the tenth day with immersion; a sign of home-coming; “the best instance of the public performance of religion and art”; large urban pavilions; traditional Bengali drumming; crowds across class, religion and ethnicity. [SRC-UNESCO-001]

**Important scope note (VERIFIED inscription vs RESEARCH-DERIVED geography).** The inscribed element is **Durga Puja in Kolkata**. Puja is also celebrated across West Bengal, India, and the Bengali diaspora. [SRC-UNESCO-002] [SRC-WBTOUR-001] This product must not claim that a BKS event is itself a UNESCO-inscribed element. It may say, with source, that Kolkata’s Durga Puja holds that inscription.

Ministry of Culture records that the Intergovernmental Committee commended involvement of marginalized groups and women in safeguarding the element. [SRC-PIB-001]

West Bengal Tourism describes the festival as the state’s biggest, a five-day homecoming from Kailash, and a cultural as well as religious event with themed pandals and an immersion carnival. [SRC-WBTOUR-001]  
**Discrepancy:** that page dates the UNESCO inscription to **15 December 2020**. UNESCO and PIB date it **15 December 2021**. [SRC-UNESCO-002] [SRC-PIB-001] Do not repeat 2020.

## 3. West Bengal traditions (B)

**RESEARCH-DERIVED.** Sharadiya (autumnal) Durga Puja in Bengal is commonly lived as:

- **Mahalaya** — end of Pitru Paksha; invocation / Chandi path in public memory; UNESCO links Mahalaya to the painting of the image’s eyes. [SRC-UNESCO-001]
- **Shashthi** — Bodhon / Kalparambha in many Bengali observances. [SRC-DRIK-001]
- **Saptami** — Nabapatrika / Kolabou (nine plants, banana plant dressed as a bride) and the opening of full public worship. [SRC-DRIK-001]
- **Ashtami** — Pushpanjali, Kumari Puja; Sandhi Puja at the Ashtami–Navami join. [SRC-DRIK-001]
- **Navami** — concluding worship / homa in many calendars. [SRC-DRIK-001]
- **Dashami** — Sindoor Khela in many Bengali contexts; visarjan / immersion. [SRC-DRIK-001] [SRC-WBTOUR-001]

Household (**bonedi bari**) Pujas and public **sarbojanin / barowari** Pujas coexist. Urban Kolkata’s theme-pandal culture is not identical to district and rural neighbourhood Pujas. [SRC-TGT-001] [SRC-CSSS-001]

**Do not** write a single “Bengal Puja” template and paste it onto an unnamed BKS venue.

## 5. Community Puja / Barowari / Sarbojanin (D)

**RESEARCH-DERIVED.** Public community Puja in Bengal is commonly described as **barowari** (historically associated with a group of twelve friends / public subscription) and later **sarbojanin** (“for all people”). UNESCO’s Tim Curtis, quoted by UN India, ties the **sarbojonin** spirit to community worship “since 1926”. [SRC-UN-INDIA-001]

This is **cultural vocabulary**, not a BKS committee type. Do not call the BKS experience “sarbojanin” unless Ram Sir and a real committee use that word.

## 7. Pandal / idol / lighting / programme ecosystem (F)

**RESEARCH-DERIVED.** Months of workshop labour: clay images; bamboo and cloth architecture; lighting; cultural programmes. [SRC-UNESCO-001] [SRC-WBTOUR-001] British Council / WB Tourism mapping treated installation, idol-making, lighting, retail, food, sponsorship, advertising as linked industries. [SRC-BRITISH-001]

Lighting and electricity load are documented for Kolkata 2019 (press summary of the study: 52 MW consumed by pujas in Kolkata that year). [SRC-HINDU-ECON-001] That figure is **2019 research**, not a 2026 BKS energy claim.

## 8. Artisan ecosystem (G)

**VERIFIED (UNESCO).** Images are sculpted in artisanal workshops from unfired Ganga clay. [SRC-UNESCO-001]

**RESEARCH-DERIVED.** Kumartuli / Kumortoli in north Kolkata is the best-documented potter neighbourhood. About 40% of idol-making studios there were older than 60 years in the 2019 mapping. Idol-making creative economy estimated ₹260–280 crore; Kolkata about ₹120 crore. [SRC-HINDU-ECON-001]

Guha-Thakurta documents a prestige gap: designers of theme pandals gained visibility; clay and bamboo workers remain essential but less celebrated. [SRC-IE-TGT-001]

**PROPOSED storytelling rule:** if BKS tells artisan stories, use the PERSON / ROLE / LOCATION / STORY / CONTRIBUTION / SOURCE / VERIFICATION / IMAGE architecture. Invented kumors are forbidden.

## 10. Bhog / community food (I)

**RESEARCH-DERIVED.** Bhog is food offered to the deity (naivedya) and then shared. Sarbojanin spreads often include khichuri, labra / mixed vegetables, beguni, tomato chutney, payesh. Onion and garlic are commonly excluded from puja cooking. House Pujas differ. [SRC-HINDU-BHOG-001]

**Agrarian thread (CULTURAL CONTEXT, not a BKS programme claim):** Nabapatrika / Kolabou binds the ritual to nine plants and the banana plant — a harvest-season vocabulary. This can sit next to Krishak Samaj **if** labelled cultural context. It must not be rewritten as “BKS integrated farming in the puja”.

**PENDING:** whether BKS serves bhog, to whom, and what the menu is.

## 12. Accessibility (K)

### Digital product (this workstream can own)

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA**: structure, contrast, keyboard, visible focus, 48px targets, captions, `prefers-reduced-motion`, bilingual `lang`. [SRC-WCAG-001] India’s RPwD Act 2016 and UNCRPD Art. 30 are the legal/ethical frame. [SRC-RPWD-001] [SRC-UNCRPD-001]

### Physical festival (reference only)

UN India and UNESCO, with IIT Kharagpur and OPDs, issued SOPs in September 2025: ramps, accessible toilets, ISL, Braille, audio, inclusive programming, emergency protocols, volunteer training. Pilot: 24 Kolkata pandals with massArt. [SRC-UNESCO-003] [SRC-UNESCO-004]

**Do not** claim ramps, accessible toilets, or quiet rooms for a BKS venue until that venue is named and audited.

## 15. Institutional festival and heritage websites (N, O, P)

Principles extracted (not copied) from Smithsonian Folklife [SRC-SFF-001] and Edinburgh International Festival [SRC-EIF-001]:

1. Lead with **people who keep the practice**, not with ornament.
2. Programme is a **data object** (date, place, audience), not a poster wall.
3. Photography is documentary and credited.
4. Navigation is short; seasonal vs permanent content is obvious.
5. Mobile is the primary visit.
6. Tone is institutional and warm, not promotional poetry.

Indian cultural institutions (IGNCA, National Museum, SNA as ICH nodal body [SRC-PIB-002]) tend toward archival calm. This product should sit closer to **Folklife + a single-community gathering** than to a theme-park landing page.

## 18. Mobile-first festival UX (S)

Festival use is almost certainly phone-in-hand, often in crowds, often on uneven networks.

Implications: one-thumb nav; large type; high contrast at night; share to WhatsApp without an SDK; no autoplay video; event cards that work at 375px; Bengali line-length; tap targets ≥ 48px.

Test viewports: 375, 390, 414, 768, 1024, 1280, 1440.
